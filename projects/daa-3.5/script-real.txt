07  {explain} The zero one knapsack. | Four items with profits ten, ten, twelve, eighteen, and weights two, four, six, nine. The bag holds fifteen.
08  {explain} First we order the items by profit per kilo: five, two and a half, two, two. | These are already in that order.
09  {emphatic} Every node gets two numbers. | An optimistic bound: fill the remaining space fractionally, like the fractional knapsack. And a guaranteed profit: whole items only.
10  {explain} At the root, items one, two and three fit: weight twelve, profit thirty two. | Three kilos are left, and a third of item four adds six. So the bounds are thirty eight and thirty two.
11  {explain} LC search always expands the live node with the best optimistic bound, | and a node is killed when its optimistic bound cannot beat the best guaranteed profit found so far.
12  {explain} Level one. Taking item one keeps the bounds at thirty eight and thirty two. | Leaving it out drops them to thirty two and twenty two. We expand the thirty eight.
13  {explain} Level two. Taking item two: still thirty eight and thirty two. | Leaving it out: thirty six and twenty two.
14  {excited} Level three. Taking item three: thirty eight and thirty two again. | But leaving item three out, item four fits whole: ten plus ten plus eighteen. Guaranteed thirty eight!
15  {excited} A guaranteed thirty eight equals the best optimistic bound anywhere, so nothing can beat it. | The answer is x equals one, one, zero, one: profit thirty eight, weight exactly fifteen.
16  {explain} Every other live node is killed: thirty six and thirty two are both below thirty eight. | Only four of the sixteen possible choices were ever examined.
17  {explain} One textbook detail. | Branch and bound minimises, so the book writes these bounds as negatives: c hat equals minus thirty eight, and u equals minus thirty two.
18  {curious} Now the travelling salesperson. | Here is the cost matrix for five cities. Infinity on the diagonal, because a city has no road to itself.
19  {explain} The lower bound comes from reducing the matrix. | Subtract the smallest entry from every row: ten, two, two, three and four. That is twenty one.
20  {explain} Then do the same for every column: one, zero, three, zero, zero, another four. | Every tour must pay at least these amounts, so c hat of the root is twenty five.
21  {explain} For each child, take a road, say one to four. | Cross out row one and column four, block the road back, four to one, and reduce again.
22  {explain} The child's c hat is the parent's twenty five, plus the reduced cost of that road, plus the new reduction. | For one to four: twenty five plus zero plus zero, twenty five.
23  {explain} The other roads from city one cost more: | one to two gives thirty five, one to three fifty three, and one to five thirty one.
24  {explain} LC search expands the smallest: one to four. | From four: to two is twenty eight, to three fifty, to five thirty six.
25  {explain} Expand one, four, two. From two: to three is fifty two, to five is twenty eight. | Expand one, four, two, five, and the only city left, three, keeps c hat at twenty eight.
26  {excited} The tour one, four, two, five, three, back to one costs twenty eight. | Every other live node is thirty one or more, so this tour is optimal.
27  {explain} Same recipe both times. | A lower bound you can trust, LC search expanding the smallest one, and bounds that kill the rest.
