VERSION A — full post (about 1,400 characters; recommended)
-----------------------------------------------------------

Ten lectures. Two units. Every algorithm traced step by step, on screen.

Every year I watch bright students lose marks in Design & Analysis of Algorithms, not because the ideas are hard, but because the textbook shows the finished table and skips the steps that produced it.

So I built a free YouTube series that shows the steps.

Units III and IV of DAA (ETCS329) are complete, 10 videos of 7 to 11 minutes each:

Unit III · Backtracking and Branch & Bound
 3.1 Backtracking fundamentals
 3.2 8-Queens and graph colouring
 3.3 Sum of subsets and Hamiltonian cycle
 3.4 Branch & bound: FIFO, LIFO and LC search
 3.5 0/1 Knapsack and TSP with branch & bound

Unit IV · NP-completeness and approximation
 4.1 Polynomial vs exponential, non-deterministic algorithms
 4.2 P, NP, NP-hard and NP-complete
 4.3 Polynomial-time reductions
 4.4 Proving NP-completeness: 3-SAT, Clique, Vertex Cover
 4.5 Approximation algorithms and their uses in AI, big data and cryptography

Every video keeps the same promise:
→ a real problem first, so the idea has a reason to exist
→ every step traced on screen: tables, trees and graphs, animated
→ proofs short enough to write in the exam
→ a practice question with a pause, then the answer
→ exam tips and a recap

There is also a lab series: the same programs written and run in both C++ and Java, live in VS Code.

Unit V, network flow, starts next.

If you teach, learn or examine algorithms, I would value your feedback. And if you know a student who has DAA this semester, please pass this on. It is free, and it will stay free.

HOW TO POST IT
--------------
1. Upload the 49-second video as a NATIVE LinkedIn video (not a YouTube link): native video autoplays in the feed and gets far more reach than an external link preview.
2. Paste Version A as the text. The first two lines show before "...see more", so they carry the hook.
3. Put the playlist link at the end of the post AND in the first comment. LinkedIn shows posts with external links to fewer people; the comment copy is the safety net.
4. Post on a weekday morning (Tuesday to Thursday, 8 to 10 am IST works well for students and faculty).
5. Reply to every comment in the first hour: early replies push the post to more feeds.
6. Three to five hashtags, no more. The ones above are specific and searched.
7. A week later, post Version B with a different frame of the video, or one lesson's thumbnail, and tag the next topic.
