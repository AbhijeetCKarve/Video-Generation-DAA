01  {curious} Imagine a computer that does one billion steps every second. | Sorting a million numbers takes it a fraction of a second.
02  {curious} Now ask it to try every tour of twenty five cities. | It will still be running ten million years from now.
03  {excited} The difference is not the computer. It is the growth rate of the algorithm, | and that one idea is the start of complexity theory.
04  {warm} Welcome to the DAA series. | I'm Abhijeet Karve, and I'll guide you through every algorithm, step by step.
05  {explain} This is Unit Four, video one. | Today: polynomial versus exponential growth, tractable and intractable problems, and deterministic versus non deterministic algorithms.
06  {explain} Non deterministic algorithms are a favourite short note in the exam, | so stay till the end for the exam tips.
07  {explain} An algorithm runs in polynomial time when its running time is bounded by a polynomial in the input size: | n, n log n, n squared, n cubed, any n to the power k.
08  {explain} It runs in exponential time when n sits in the exponent, | like two to the n, or n factorial. These grow much, much faster.
09  {emphatic} Watch the table, at a billion steps a second. | n cubed for n equals one hundred takes one millisecond. Two to the n for n equals one hundred takes forty trillion years.
10  {explain} The point is the gap. Make the computer a thousand times faster, | and a cubic algorithm handles ten times the input, but an exponential one handles only about ten more items.
11  {explain} Problems that have a polynomial time algorithm are called tractable. | Problems for which no polynomial time algorithm is known are called intractable.
12  {explain} Sorting, searching, shortest paths, the minimum spanning tree: all tractable. | The travelling salesperson, Hamiltonian cycle, graph coloring, the zero one knapsack: no polynomial algorithm is known for any of them.
13  {explain} Those are exactly the problems from Unit Three. | Backtracking and branch and bound help on average, but in the worst case they still take exponential time.
14  {curious} Nobody has proved that these problems have no polynomial algorithm. Nobody has found one either. | That open question is P versus NP, the subject of the next video.
15  {explain} Every algorithm so far has been deterministic: | each step has exactly one outcome, and the same input always follows the same path.
16  {excited} A non deterministic algorithm is allowed one magical step: Choice. | Choice picks an element from a set, and if any pick can lead to success, it picks that one.
17  {explain} Three functions. Choice of S picks an element of the set S. Failure signals an unsuccessful completion. Success signals a successful one. | Each takes order one time.
18  {emphatic} The rule: if there is any sequence of choices that leads to Success, the algorithm is assumed to make exactly those choices. | It fails only when no sequence of choices can succeed.
19  {explain} Example one: searching. j is Choice of one to n. | If A of j equals x, write j and Success. Otherwise, Failure.
20  {explain} Deterministically, search takes n steps in the worst case. | Non deterministically, it takes order one: the position is guessed, and one comparison checks it.
21  {explain} Example two: sorting positive integers. For each i, guess a position j with Choice of one to n. If B of j is already filled, Failure. Else B of j gets A of i. | Then check: if any B of i is bigger than B of i plus one, Failure. Otherwise write B, and Success.
22  {explain} Every loop runs at most n times, so non deterministic sorting is order n, | where the best deterministic sort needs n log n.
23  {explain} Two ways to picture it. A machine with unlimited parallelism that tries every choice at the same time, | or a lucky guesser whose guess is then verified. Either way: guess, then verify.
24  {explain} Example three: the zero one knapsack as a yes or no question. Is there a choice of items with weight at most m and profit at least r? | Guess each x of i as zero or one with Choice, add up the weight and profit, and check. Order n.
25  {emphatic} That is the pattern behind every hard problem from Unit Three. | Finding the answer looks exponential, but checking a given answer is polynomial.
26  {explain} That gives the class NP: non deterministic polynomial time. | A yes or no problem is in NP when a non deterministic algorithm solves it in polynomial time: guess the answer, verify it fast.
27  {curious} Your turn. | Write a non deterministic algorithm that decides whether a graph on n vertices has a Hamiltonian cycle, in polynomial time.

29  {excited} Guess: for each position i, v of i is Choice of one to n. | Verify: no vertex repeats, every consecutive pair is an edge, and the last vertex connects back to the first. That check is polynomial, so Hamiltonian cycle is in NP.
30  {emphatic} Exam tip one. | Define a polynomial time algorithm by the bound order n to the k, and give the growth table: n cubed against two to the n.
31  {emphatic} Exam tip two. | For non deterministic algorithms, name the three functions, Choice, Failure and Success, and state the rule: the algorithm fails only when no sequence of choices succeeds.
32  {emphatic} Exam tip three. | Write non deterministic search or sorting as your example, with its time: order one for search, order n for sorting.
33  {warm} To recap: | polynomial time is tractable, exponential time is not, | the hard problems of Unit Three have no known polynomial algorithm, | and a non deterministic algorithm guesses with Choice and verifies in polynomial time.
